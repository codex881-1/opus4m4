# DOSSIER — The Manchuria air-pickup, 29 November 1952
**Subject:** CIA officers John T. Downey and Richard G. Fecteau, shot down over Jilin Province, China; imprisoned until 1971 / 1973.
**Prepared for:** 70-second documentary short (fact-check lead). **Research date:** 28 Oct 2026.
**Method:** Every claim below was checked against at least one primary or near-primary text that I actually opened (not only search snippets). Where a source could not be opened, it is listed under "Not accessed". Wikipedia and search-engine summaries were used only as pointers.

## 2. CLAIM-BY-CLAIM

### Claim 1 — Date, time, airfield, airline, aircraft, markings
**Status: CONFIRMED (with nuances).**
- **Date:** evening of **29 November 1952** (a Saturday). *"Late on 29 November, Downey and Fecteau boarded Schwartz and Snoddy's olive drab C-47 on an airfield on the Korean peninsula"* [D06]. CIA film: *"At 21:40, their unmarked plane took off heading due north"* [CIA-Film].
- **Airfield:** *"left K-16 airfield near Seoul, South Korea, on the evening of November 29, 1952"* [D22]; *"departed K-16 airfield near Seoul, South Korea"* [DPAA]. (Leary says only "departed Seoul" [Leary94].) In the Korean War the USAF designated Seoul's Yeouido airfield "K-16" (Kimpo was K-14) — Wikipedia pointer citing the USAF Museum K-base list that Dujmovic's fn 4 links to; I did not open the USAF list itself.
- **Airline:** Civil Air Transport (CAT), "the CIA's proprietary" airline [D06]. Dujmovic's wording is "proprietary" — safest English: "an airline the CIA secretly controlled."
- **Aircraft:** Douglas **C-47**, "olive drab," **unmarked** on the mission [D06; CIA-Film; Foreign Affairs: "unmarked C-47"].
- **Tail number:** DPAA report: *"a CAT C-47, tail number B-813"* [DPAA]. **Caution:** the CIA says the plane carried no markings that night, and a *second* C-47 was "re-marked with the registration number of the missing C-47" for the 3 Dec cover flight [D22]. So B-813 is the aircraft's *registration of record*, not something to paint visibly on the fuselage.
- **Flight:** ~400 miles, "a quiet, uneventful flight of less than three hours" [D06]. Over the target "around midnight" (Downey: "12, 12:15, on time" [CIA-Film]).
- **Time nuance:** the aircraft reached the area ~midnight, dropped supplies, left ~45 min, returned [D06] → ambush roughly 00:45–01:00 on 30 Nov Korean time. A Chinese villager told the 2002 US team the plane came "around midnight on 29 November 1952" [DPAA]. Chinese officer Wu: "22:45" [Wu02]. **These do not reconcile (time-zone/recollection). Use "late that night."**

### Claim 2 — The pilots
**Status: CONFIRMED.**
- **Norman A. Schwartz**, pilot, "raised in a working-class neighborhood in Louisville, Kentucky," *"was 29 years old when he died in the line of duty"* [CIA-Hero]. **Robert C. Snoddy**, co-pilot, learned to fly in Oregon, *"was 31 years old"* [CIA-Hero]; DPAA gives hometown Roseburg, Oregon [DPAA-ID]; CIA says remains were buried in his mother's plot in Eugene, Oregon [CIA-Hero].
- Both were CAT volunteers who had trained in the pickup technique in fall 1952 [D06]. (The CIA film adds that Air Force pilots had pulled out over the risk.)
- **Fate:** *"Whether due to gunfire, the impact, or the fire, the pilots died at the scene."* [D06]. Chinese villager in 2002 found "two bodies near … the cockpit area," buried under thin soil because "the ground was frozen" [DPAA]. After 2002 and June 2004 joint excavations, remains were identified as **Snoddy's** (CIA: 24 Mar 2005; DPAA release 31 Mar 2005). *"To date, efforts … have not identified any remains associated with Norman Schwartz."* [CIA-Hero]. Stars on the CIA Memorial Wall in **1998** [CIA-Hero].
- Chinese state media call Snoddy the "navigator" [Wu02] — wrong; he was co-pilot.

### Claim 3 — Ages and backgrounds
**Status: CONFIRMED / CORRECTED for press errors.**
- **Downey:** born **19 April 1930**, Wallingford, Connecticut → **22 years 7 months** on 29 Nov 1952. [D06: *"John Downey, 22 when he began his captivity and almost 43 when released"*]. Yale B.A. (English) 1951; football, wrestling, rugby. Joined CIA June 1951 [D06].
- **Fecteau:** born **August 1927**, Lynn, Massachusetts → **25** on the flight. [D06: *"Richard Fecteau, 25 when shot down and 44 on his return, will be 80 next August"* (written 2006)]. Boston University (BU Class of 1951 [Wood18]; had first attended the US Merchant Marine Academy). Joined CIA a few months after Downey; on the flight only ~3–4 weeks after arriving in Asia [D06; Wood18].
- **First overseas assignment and first operational mission** for both [D06].
- **Role before the flight:** Downey had been at the unit ~a year and had *trained the agents*; Fecteau arrived first week of November [D06]. Downey and Fecteau were in the CIA's training unit in Japan (Atsugi area per Chinese sources).
- **Press errors to avoid:** Chinese indictment: 24 and 27 [PD20]; AP/TIME 1971: Fecteau 43 [AP71; TIME71] (he was 44); publisher blurb: Downey 23.

### Claim 4 — The agent team, how it was captured, who held the radio
**Status: team facts CONFIRMED; capture date UNCERTAIN.**
- **CIA side [D06]:** *"The second Third Force team comprised five ethnic Chinese dropped into the Jilin region of Manchuria in mid-July 1952. Downey was well known to the Chinese operatives on this team because he had trained them. The team quickly established radio contact … and was resupplied by air in August and October. A sixth team member, intended as a courier … was dropped in September. In early November, the team reported contact with a local dissident leader and said it had obtained needed operational documents such as official credentials. They requested air-exfiltration of the courier, a method he had trained for but that the CIA had never attempted operationally."* 20 Nov: CIA radioed *"Will air snatch approximately 2400 hours"* on 29 Nov. First Third Force team (4 men, April 1952, south China) "was never heard from again."
- **No CIA codename** for the team found. Leary: Manchuria drops were "part of a project known to the pilots as Operation Tropic" [Leary94]. Chinese sources call it the **"Wen team" (文队)**, after leader alias Wen Shijie. The agents were trained on **Saipan** [FRUS-54 Doc 415; Delury abstract] under the "Third Force" / "Free China Movement" (自由中国运动) program.
- **Scale of failure:** *"of 212 Third Force agents dropped into China during the Korean War period, 111 were captured and 101 killed"* [FA23, citing CIA records via Delury].
- **When captured/turned — three versions:**
  1. **CIA Far East Division (later assessment):** *"probably had been caught and doubled immediately after its insertion in July"* [D06 fn 5].
  2. **Delury (Chinese archives):** the July infiltration *"escaped detection, but discovery of the liaison Li Junying triggered a Manchurian manhunt"* [Delury22 abstract].
  3. **Chinese officer Wu Hengmo [Wu02], my translation:** June—lights and a US-marked canvas reported; **21 Sept** courier **Li Junying (alias 卜经武 "Pu Ching-wu"; 44, from Liaoyang)** air-dropped, found nothing, and on **29 Sept gave himself up** to Antu County public security; **2 Nov** team leader Zhang Zaiwen (alias Wen Shijie) seized and agent Man Chunhui shot dead by a Chinese scout; **13 Nov, 21:30** radio technician **Niu Songlin (牛松林)** captured. A resupply drop came next day (6 cases of food, a letter of instructions, spare radio, new code cards). *"Following the new code card, the captured radio technician re-established contact with the CIA's Japan station"* and sent: *"Li Junying has completed all tasks; plans to board a plane at 23:00 on 29 Nov at the south gully of Sandaogou to return to Tokyo to report; request an air pickup; signal: three fires in a '品' (triangle) shape, plus a direction-finder"*; CIA replied on **26 Nov**: "Pick-up as per your time and place; be ready."
- **Who controlled the radio on the night:** the Chinese. [FA23]: *"the Communists had captured a Third Force radio operator and turned him, promising leniency if he continued to radio the Americans … and reassure them that all was normal."* [D06]: *"The request for exfiltration was a ruse."*
- **Conflicts:** CIA says "early November" messages and a 20 Nov reply; Chinese say radio man caught 13 Nov, reply 26 Nov, and resupply 14 Nov (CIA says resupplies were August/October). Chinese signal geometry ("品" triangle) vs. Downey's memory "three in a straight line" vs. Fecteau's "flashlight" [D06 fn 7: *"Both were used."*]. **Do not show a specific fire pattern as fact.**
- **The man in the harness:** Chinese account says provincial public-security scout **Zhang Zhenbang (张振邦)** posed as Li Junying in the pickup harness, "the rope cut as a precaution" [Wu02]. CIA says only *"One man was in the pickup harness"* [D06], and that after capture Downey saw **the real courier in a village building, nodding at a Chinese officer who said, in English, "You are Jack."** The two accounts are compatible. Use only if attributed to Chinese sources.

### Claim 5 — How the air-pickup system worked
**Status: CONFIRMED; some numbers conflict.**
- **[D06]:** *"the technique for aerial pickup involved flying an aircraft at low altitude and hooking a line elevated between two poles. The line was connected to a harness in which the agent was strapped. Once airborne, the man was to be winched into the aircraft."* Required "specialized training … for the two men who would operate the winch."
- **System name:** the "All American" system, adapted by the Army Air Force in WWII from a mail-bag pickup; **not** Fulton's Skyhook (1960s) [D06 fn 4]. (All American Aviation → All American Engineering, Wilmington, DE.)
- **Poles and line:** Leary: *"two steel poles, set 54 feet apart, with a transfer line strung between them."* CIA 2026: *"two 15-foot poles set in the ground about 50-feet apart with a line strung between them."* **Both agree on ~50–54 ft spacing; use "two poles, about 50 feet apart"; pole height 15 ft per CIA 2026.**
- **Aircraft side:** Leary: *"a flight mechanic paid out a 50-foot steel cable … a four-finger grapple at the end of the cable engaged the transfer rope, shock absorbers cushioned the impact, and then the flight mechanic winched the … pouch on board"*; approach in "a gentle glide of 90 mph" (mail pickups) [Leary94]. CIA honoring page: *"a hook extended out the plane's back door that would snag a line between two upright poles … The agent would be connected to the line by a harness. Once the hook caught the line, the agent would be jerked off the ground."* Fecteau: *"You come in about 50 feet off the ground with a pole that's extended from the plane and with a hook on it."* [CIA-Film].
- **Speed on the fatal pass:** *"flying nearly at its stall speed of around 60 knots"* [D06; CIA-Hero: "only 60 knots"].
- **Altitude:** ~50 ft (Fecteau, [CIA-Film]); Chinese account: "under 20 m" [Wu02].
- **Tested on people?** Yes. First human pickup **5 Sept 1943**: Lt. Alex Doster, paratrooper, *"yanked vertically off the ground, then soared off behind the aircraft. It took less than three minutes to retrieve him."* (Stinson at 125 mph); instrument tests had logged **17 g**, cut to **7 g** by harness/line changes; *"The first live test, with a sheep, failed when the harness twisted and strangled the animal."* [Leary94]. In fall 1952 CAT pilots in Japan *"made a number of static pickups, then successfully retrieved mechanic Ronald E. Lewis"* [Leary94]. Fecteau, on rehearsing with the two pilots: *"We did it several times, no problem at all."* [CIA-Film]. **But:** *"a method … the CIA had never attempted operationally"* [D06].
- **Downey and Fecteau's role:** the **winch operators** at the rear door. Originally Chinese crewmen were to do it; then two CAT men were trained but *"the CIA unit chief pulled them four days before the mission because they lacked the requisite clearances. Downey … and Fecteau … were directed to fill the breach. They were hurriedly trained in the procedure during the week of 24 November."* [D06]. They were harnessed to the aircraft "to keep them from falling out during the winching."

### Claim 6 — The ambush
**Status: CONFIRMED; Chinese version differs.**
- **Place:** Jilin Province, near **Antu** County in the Changbai Mountains. [D06: *"possibly a police station in Antu, which was near the pickup point"*]. [DPAA]: crash *"approximately 12 km southeast of Sandao, Jilin Province"*; excavation datum **N 42°29′06.6″ E 128°29′40.3″**; debris field ~20 m × 30 m. Chinese: **三道沟花砬子 (Sandaogou, Huala'zi)**, Antu [Wu02].
- **Sequence [D06]:** arrival ~midnight; recognition signal received; Downey and Fecteau "pushed out supplies" (food and equipment for the pickup); plane left ~45 min to let the team set poles; returned, received "ready" signal; **first pass = dry run** (to orient pilots and alert the man); co-pilot Snoddy came back to check the two officers; on the moonlit snow "four or five people could be seen," one in the harness; **second pass:** *"white sheets that had been camouflaging two antiaircraft guns on the snowy terrain flew off and gunfire erupted at the very moment the pickup was to have been made. The guns, straddling the flight path, began a murderous crossfire. At this point, a crowd of men emerged from the woods."* Pilots pulled the nose up; engines cut; *"the aircraft glided to a controlled crash among some trees, breaking in two with the nose in the air."*
- **Fire type:** CIA: two anti-aircraft guns. Fecteau: *"two American 50s"* [CIA-Film] (.50-cal). Gunfire came through the cockpit floor; tracers ignited fuel; one bullet "singed Downey's cheek" [D06].
- **What happened to each man:**
  - Schwartz and Snoddy: died at the scene (above).
  - Downey and Fecteau: harnessed; slid along the floor. *"Fecteau's harness broke, causing him to crash into the bulkhead … a bump on his head 'you could hang your coat on.'"* Both tried to reach the cockpit; fire forced them back. Stunned, they stood outside telling each other they were *"in a hell of a mess."* Chinese forces came *"whooping and hollering."* [D06].
- **Chinese version [PD20; Wu02; Sina08]:** hundreds of PLA and public-security troops in white capes; deputy commander **Tan Youlin (谭友林)** in command; an AA machine-gun battalion and an infantry battalion on the hills; Wu's battalion with "36 machine guns"; flare as the hook was about to catch; "hundreds" of guns fired; plane pulled up, then plunged and hit a ridge; Americans "bailed out" and landed in deep snow. **Dujmovic fn 8:** a 2000 Chinese book claims *"37 guns … and 400 armed security forces"* and that the Americans "came out firing" — which he calls "highly fanciful." **Trust the survivors + DPAA debris field + CIA** over the Chinese "bailed out" claim (Wu's own text says they were too low to open parachutes).
- **Capture [D06]:** taken to a village building (possibly Antu police station), where the courier nodded to a Chinese officer in a leather jacket with a pistol, who said in English *"You are Jack."* Fecteau was told *"Your future is very dark."* Fecteau gave his **full name "Richard George Fecteau"** deliberately — to warn off rescuers if the Chinese sent a fake message in his name. Then truck + train, a dozen armed guards, to **Mukden (Shenyang), "almost 300 miles away,"** leg irons, separate cells.

### Claim 7 — US cover story, what families were told, "presumed dead"
**Status: CONFIRMED.**
- **[D06]:** *"when the C-47 was overdue for its return on the morning of 30 November 1952, CIA worked with Civil Air Transport to concoct a cover story—a CAT aircraft on a commercial flight from Korea to Japan on 3 December was missing and, as of 4 December, was presumed lost in the Sea of Japan. Downey and Fecteau were identified as Department of the Army civilian employees."* DCI Walter Bedell Smith signed condolence letters: *"I have learned that [your son/your husband] was a passenger on a commercial plane flight between South Korea and Japan which is now overdue and that there is grave fear that he may have been lost."*
- **Cover flight [D22]:** a second CAT C-47 *"re-marked with the registration number of the missing C-47, openly took off from the K-16 airfield late on December 3rd"*; manifest listed Schwartz and Snoddy as pilots and Downey and Fecteau as passengers; actual pilot **Hugh Marsh** flew alone and returned in darkness. Same day (3 Dec) President-elect Eisenhower began his Korea visit [D22].
- **Wrinkle:** both men had been told before the flight to say they were **CAT employees**, which clashed with the cover story of "Army civilians" — Chinese interrogators exploited the mismatch [D06; D22].
- **Presumed dead:** *"declared Downey and Fecteau 'presumed dead' on 4 December 1953. Letters to that effect were sent to the families under the signature of DCI Allen Dulles."* (Dujmovic fn 11: exactly a year and a day after the cover-story loss date.) Mid-Dec 1952 official status was "missing in action" [D06].
- **State Department language, 23 Nov 1954:** *"Civilians are Department Army employees John T. Downey and George Fecteau missing in flight November 1952 Korea to Japan"* [FRUS-54 Doc 406]. (State misnamed Fecteau "George"; his name was Richard George.)

### Claim 8 — China's announcement, trial, sentences
**Status: CONFIRMED (date 23 Nov 1954). Trial description conflicts.**
- **[D06]:** *"That day, 23 November 1954, almost a year after the CIA had pronounced Downey and Fecteau 'presumed dead,' Beijing declared them alive, in custody, and serving their sentences as convicted CIA spies. The first that the Agency learned of it was through a New China News Agency broadcast."* Same announcement covered the 11-man USAF B-29 crew (shot down 12/13 Jan 1953).
- **Sentences:** *"Downey received life imprisonment; Fecteau, 20 years."* Downey = *"Chief Culprit,"* Fecteau = *"Assistant Chief Culprit."* [D06]. Nine Chinese agents tried with them; four got death [D22]; Downey's memory of the verdict reading: *"Life, life, death, life, death"* [CIA-Film].
- **FRUS Doc 415 (30 Nov 1954):** Indian Embassy translation of the Chinese decrees: *"first case involved two airmen named Downey and Fecteau and nine Chinese… shot down over northeast China November 29, 1952. … Both reported to have confessed that they came to China to 'pick up agent Pu Ching Wu and take him back to Japan.' Downey allegedly admitted his employment by CIA…"* (Decrees printed in *People's China*, 16 Dec 1954.)
- **Chinese state-media description [PD20], my translation:** the **Military Division of the Supreme People's Court** held a *"public trial"* on 23 Nov 1954 (presiding judge Jia Qian 贾潜; prosecutor Yao Lun 姚伦; public defender Zhao Xilun 赵希伦). Dujmovic calls it *"a secret military proceeding."* Downey himself: *"it was fair to call it a show trial. It wasn't a trial as we would understand it."* [CIA-Film]. **Say "a military court in Beijing" and avoid "secret" or "public."**
- **First American reaction (contemporary):** UP 24 Nov 1954: *"News of the sentencing … was their families' first evidence that they were still alive."* Mary Downey had had *"no hope whatsoever"* until the news [UP54].
- **A tell-tale vivid fact:** Fecteau whispered *"Who's your tailor?"* to Downey in the courtroom; Downey smiled thinly [D06; CIA-Film]. Their first sight of each other in two years.

### Claim 9 — Chinese offers of release and Washington's response
**Status: CORRECTED/NUANCED.**
- **Linkage to US journalists:** the Chinese invited 18 US newsmen in Aug 1956 [TIME57]. Dulles told reporters (5 Feb 1957): *"the Chinese Communist government has for some time been trying to get reporters, preferably those it picked, to come into Communist China, and it has repeatedly tried to use the illegal detention of Americans … as a means of pressure … That kind of blackmail I don't propose to satisfy."* [TIME57, quoting the press conference]. Eisenhower the next day: *"This is a new thought that has come in."* [TIME57].
- **Internal US record [FRUS-57 Doc 237, 18 Feb 1957, Dulles to press leaders]:** *"the assertion by the Communist press after the failure to achieve a Dulles–Chou En-lai meeting that they, the Communists, would be more lenient when the U.S. attitude on cultural exchanges was revised. The clear implication … was that if we let newspapermen into Communist China … it would constitute a revision sufficient to warrant the release of the prisoners."* Dulles quoted a 14 Feb 1957 Chinese statement at Geneva that the US press "had already done the job for them."
- **Scholarship:** Rubin: Zhou's "newsmen-prisoner exchange offer … emerged in February 1957" (Dulles said a similar offer had been made in 1955 re the B-29 crew). **FA23/Delury** date the offer to **1956** and add that China also demanded the US "admit that Downey and Fecteau were indeed CIA agents."
- **Washington's response:** refused ("blackmail"); kept the "Army civilians" fiction. In **late 1957** the US *did* lift its ban on prisoners' relatives travelling to China (FRUS-57 Doc 294 of 16 Nov 1957 and Supplement Doc 845 of 26 Nov 1957; Rubin) — that is how Mary Downey got to Beijing in Jan 1958. CIA itself (Bissell, 1954–55) pressed for action; State/Pentagon would not include the CIA men in the 1955 military release [D06].
- **1983 follow-up (snippet only):** UPI 3 Sep 1983: Downey "has been told by Chinese officials the United States rejected a proposal that would have freed him after five years."
- **Safe wording:** "In the 1950s Beijing signalled it might be lenient if Washington let American reporters visit; Secretary of State Dulles called that blackmail and refused."

### Claim 11 — Prisons, conditions, solitary
**Status: PARTLY CONFIRMED; prison names NOT found.**
- **Mukden (Shenyang)** for ~5 months, then **a prison in Beijing** (CIA film says spring 1953) [D06; CIA-Film]. **No source I opened names the Beijing prison.** (TIME73 says "a mazelike prison outside Peking" — second-hand, unsourced.) Interrogations up to 24 h; sleep deprivation; *"never tortured physically or, after their initial capture, beaten."* Downey confessed CIA affiliation on the 16th day; Fecteau gave a "cover confession" on the 13th day, giving **names of Boston University football teammates** as supposed CIA classmates [D06].
- **"The whipsaw"**: captors improved conditions then took them away. In 1955 they lived three weeks with the B-29 crew and believed they would go home — *"then, as Downey recalls, 'the axe fell'"* and they were returned to solitary [D06].
- **Study of Marx/Lenin/Mao** ~1959–1969 [D06]. **Monthly letters/packages** after year three [D06 fn 19].
- **Coping:** schedules, exercise, Fecteau's "daydreaming" (remembering every sixth-grade classmate; mental movies), Downey's calisthenics and prayer; coughs, dust-written scores and later notes between the two [D06]. Downey: *"500 blank pages"*; Fecteau: *"boring."*
- **Caveat:** TIME73 quotes ex-POW Kiba on 'white stones in the rice gruel' — not corroborated; skip.

### Claim 13 — Nixon's admission (verbatim)
**Status: CONFIRMED + trap.** *The President's News Conference, 31 January 1973, 11:25 a.m., White House Briefing Room (APP, "AMERICAN PRISONERS IN CHINA," item [13]).*
> **Q.** "Mr. President, there are two American fliers still being held prisoner in China, and they are sort of in limbo-well, three Americans but two fliers. I wonder if you could give us their status, and do you expect them to be returned with the other prisoners?"
> **THE PRESIDENT.** "This matter we discussed when we were in the People's Republic of China, and we have every reason to believe that these fliers will be released on the initiative of the People's Republic of China as the POW situation is worked out in Vietnam. I won't go beyond that because this is a matter that should be left to the People's Republic of China, but we have, we believe, every assurance that will happen."
> **Q.** "Downey, also?"  *(Q/A paragraph breaks in the transcript are collapsed below where noted [¶].)*
> **THE PRESIDENT.** "**Downey is a different case, as you know. Downey involves a CIA agent. His sentence of 30 years has been, I think, commuted to 5 years, and we have also discussed that with Premier Chou En-lai.** I would have to be quite candid: We have no assurance that any change of action, other than the commutation of the sentence, will take place, but we have, of course, informed the People's Republic through our private channels that we feel that would be a very salutary action on his part. [¶] But that is a matter where they must act on their own initiative, and it is not one where any public pressures or bellicose statements from here will be helpful in getting his release."
- **Traps:** (a) Nixon said **"30 years"; Downey's sentence was life** (reduced Dec 1971 to 5 years from that date); (b) the phrase is "involves a CIA agent," not "is a CIA agent" (Rubin notes NYT headlined that Nixon "conceded … that [Downey] was an agent of the C.I.A."); (c) he said it as an aside at the very end of a Vietnam-peace press conference.
- **Background:** CIA's Helms told Kissinger on 9 Sept 1971 that an admission *"would not now present serious security problems"* [FRUS-71 Doc 164 fn 15]. Kissinger told Nixon (Nov 1971): *"They indeed were [CIA agents], and CIA, for its part, would be willing for us to admit their activities…"* [Doc 164]. Nixon to Zhou, 25 Feb 1972: *"We know that Downey was guilty."* [Doc 200].

### Claim 15 — Pay, and what became of each man
**Status: CONFIRMED.**

Funds invested in savings bonds, pseudonymous accounts, then (1964, DCI McCone) a covert proprietary company. *"When Fecteau was released in 1971, his accumulated account came to almost $140,000; Downey's in 1973 came to more than $170,000."* They still thought in terms of their 1952 salary (just over **$4,000**); GS-14 pay was ~$22,000. DCI Helms to the Senate, 1973: *"their salaries were paid just as though they were on our rolls"* [Rubin fn 186]. (TIME73's "$350,000" for Downey is not supported by CIA records.)

Downey Courthouse** (New Haven Juvenile Matters Courthouse renamed 2002 [CT Public 2014]); married Audrey Lee (born in Manchuria) in 1975; died **17 Nov 2014**, aged 84 [CT Public; D06].

### Claim 17 — Decision-level detail
**Status: CONFIRMED (myth corrected).**
- **Who approved the men's presence:** Downey's CIA **unit chief**, tactically fixing a security-clearance problem; *"it was only after the shootdown that the rules were changed so that no CIA officer would fly over the Chinese mainland."* Fn 10: *"the participation of CIA officers on overflights of denied areas was to be minimized, [but] local field commanders were allowed to so decide on their own discretion."* No inquiry, no discipline [D06].
- **Myth:** *"One of the most persistent myths was that the two must have been joyriding … In fact, the record shows that they were directed to be on the flight and that they had received specialized training."* [D06]. CIA film (Dujmovic): *"It was said that they were on a joyride … none of this is true."*
- **Policy-level authority for the program (not the specific flight):** FRUS-54 Doc 435 attachment: JCS recommended guerrilla action on the mainland (~10 July 1950); State approved a CIA dispatch (20 July 1950); NSC 10/5 (Oct 1951) *"authorized the conduct of expanded guerrilla activities within China."* The memo called the mission "pursuant to a specific request of the Joint Chiefs of Staff, and approved as to policy by the State Department."
- **Warnings ignored?** A former senior officer (unnamed) says that in summer 1952 two team messages made him *"90 percent"* sure the team was doubled; he told the unit chief, was rebuffed, persisted, and was transferred; after the shoot-down the chief told him not to talk. **Uncorroborated; "no record of an inquiry … appears to exist."** The Aug/Oct resupply successes could be read as evidence the team was clean [D06]. Downey bore no bitterness: *"I felt for him. It turned out to be such a goddamned disaster from his point of view."*
- **Official US stance in Dec 1954:** disclosing the real mission *"would be highly questionable"* because the case wasn't "airtight" [FRUS-54 Doc 435]. CIA briefly considered a commando raid but lacked location data [D06 fn 16].

## 5. SAFE NARRATION FACTS (each sentence is supported; safe to say aloud)

1. On the night of 29 November 1952, a plane took off from an airfield near Seoul and flew north into China. *[D22; DPAA; CIA-Film]*
2. It was an unmarked C-47, flown by an airline the CIA secretly controlled. *[D06; CIA-Film; Foreign Affairs]*
3. Two American pilots flew it: Norman Schwartz and Robert Snoddy. *[CIA-Hero]*
4. In the back were two young CIA officers: John Downey, 22, and Richard Fecteau, 25. *[D06; Wood18]*
5. It was their first mission. *[D06]*
6. They were there to pick up a Chinese agent, without landing. *[D06; CIA-Hero]*
7. On the ground, two poles held a line. The plane would trail a hook, catch the line, and winch the man aboard. *[D06; Leary94; CIA-Prelude]*
8. Downey and Fecteau had been trained, just days before, to work the winch. *[D06]*

## 6. FIVE MOST CINEMATIC VERIFIED DETAILS
1. **White sheets on snow.** *"white sheets that had been camouflaging two antiaircraft guns on the snowy terrain flew off and gunfire erupted at the very moment the pickup was to have been made"* — under a nearly full moon, with the man in the harness facing the plane [D06].
2. **The empty pistol joke.** Fecteau finds the survival-kit .32 has no bullets; the only words spoken on the flight [D06].
3. **"You are Jack."** The captured courier nods; a man in a leather jacket points at Downey and says it in English. Fecteau deliberately gives his *full* name to warn rescuers [D06].
4. **Phantom plane.** On 3 Dec a second C-47, re-marked with the lost plane's registration, takes off from K-16 with one pilot (Hugh Marsh), a manifest listing the dead pilots and the two prisoners, and returns in the dark — to build a cover story. Same day, Eisenhower is touring Korea [D22].
5. **The salute and the ping-pong match.** Told he is being freed, Downey asks to finish watching a table-tennis match on Chinese TV; at the Hong Kong border a British soldier salutes him — *"the first act of dignity shown him in 20 years"* [D06]. (Close runner-up: Kissinger telephoned Fecteau's father; Philip Fecteau hung up, thinking it a hoax — *"the man had a German accent."* [TIME71])

## 7. THE CHINESE-SIDE ACCOUNT, CONDENSED (my translation; attribute as "Chinese state-media account")
From [Wu02] and [PD20]:
- Chinese forces found an air-dropped team in the Changbai Mountains; the courier **Li Junying** surrendered on 29 Sept 1952 and revealed four drops of agents; the **"Wen team"** in the **Laoling** hills was hunted down in October–November; the radio technician **Niu Songlin** was captured on 13 Nov and made to transmit on the Chinese side's orders.
- Chinese message to the CIA: Li had finished his tasks and wished to be picked up on 29 Nov at 23:00 at **Sandaogou**; the US replied that it would send a plane.
- On 29 Nov hundreds of troops in white capes lay on the hills; a provincial public-security scout sat in the pickup frame as "Li" (rope cut as a precaution); three fires lit. At 22:45 an unmarked C-47 appeared; on its third circuit, under 20 m up, a flare went up and the guns opened fire.
- Downey and Fecteau were taken alive; their IDs showed they were instructors from the CIA training facility at **Atsugi, Japan**. The Chinese government commended the units; Wu Hengmo was cited for merit.
- 23 Nov 1954: military court of the Supreme People's Court tries "Downey and Fecteau" in public; Downey life, Fecteau 20 years.
- **Handle with care:** claims of a mass of guns, of the men parachuting, of Zhou citing the case at the 1955 Asian–African conference, and of Fecteau's mother's 1955 visit are unsupported or contradicted by Western primary sources.

## 11. DESIGN-ACCURACY NOTES (verified props and setting, for visuals)
- **Aircraft:** olive-drab Douglas C-47, no insignia; rear cargo door used for hook/winch; crew of 4 (2 pilots, 2 winch operators in harnesses). [D06; CIA-Film]
- **Pickup rig:** two ~15-ft poles ~50 ft apart, line between, man in parachute-type harness facing the aircraft's path; trailing hook/grapple on a steel cable; shock absorber; winch. [Leary94; CIA-Prelude; D06]
- **Terrain/season:** snow-covered forested hills, frozen ground, near-full moon high overhead, bonfires/flashlight at the pick-up zone, camouflaged guns under white sheets straddling the flight path, 60 knots, "very low." [D06; DPAA]
- **Clothing:** "heavy winter clothing" on the officers [D06]; Chinese troops in white capes/felt boots (Chinese account) [Wu02].
- **Captors' tone:** "whooping and hollering" [D06]; officer with leather jacket and pistol [D06].
- **Cell:** ~5×8 ft, four cement walls, thick wooden door, whitewashed window, a single bulb burning day and night, small eyehole with a sliding wooden cover, straw mattress [D06; CIA-Film].
- **Release:** Lo Wu bridge (Shenzhen–Hong Kong border), Mao-style blue suit and cap (Fecteau) [AP71: "blue Chinese suit with blue cap"]; RAF helicopter to Kai Tak; British soldier's salute (Downey).
